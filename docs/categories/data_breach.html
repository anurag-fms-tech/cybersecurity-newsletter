Philippine Migrant Worker Data Likely Leaked in 69GB Government Breach

Severity: 9/10

An unknown actor exfiltrated roughly 69 GB of Philippine Overseas Employment Administration data in under 30 minutes, copying 27 SQL Server backup databases that contained worker, contract and financial records. Hunt.io traced the breach to a Singapore staging server, noting the use of privilege‑escalation tools (GodPotato, SeImpersonate) and Impacket. While the data’s ultimate destination is unclear, the breach is confirmed; attribution remains uncertain, though Chinese language fragments were tied to a third‑party library. This follows earlier thefts of Philippine government data.

AI-Linked Breaches Mount as WorkOS, Northflank, Trigger.dev Disclose Hacks

Severity: 7/10

Hackers are increasingly stealing AI access tokens to monetize them, using AI‑assisted tools to automate intrusions across more than a dozen developer platforms—including CommandCode, Northflank, Trigger.dev, Chainstack, and WorkOS. The attacks vary—compromised credentials, third‑party dependencies, and exploits—but all target stored customer secrets. Analysts link the surge to “LLM‑jacking” and broker markets, noting repeated use of the open‑source Artex framework and suggesting a growing credential‑theft economy.

Student Loan Breach Exposes 2.5M Records

Severity: 8/10

A breach at Nelnet Servicing, the loan‑servicing platform for EdFinancial and the Oklahoma Student Loan Authority, exposed personal data of 2.5 million student‑loan borrowers. Between June 1 and July 22 2022, unauthorized parties accessed names, addresses, emails, phone numbers and Social Security numbers (financial data remained safe). Nelnet detected the vulnerability on July 21, disclosed it on August 17, and engaged third‑party forensics.
