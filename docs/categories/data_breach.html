Aussies Face Reduced Cybercrime Risk, as Pressure Shifts to SMBs

Severity: 2 This news suggests a reduction in cybersecurity threats but does not specifically indicate a severe new or existing threat, keeping severity low to moderate./10

Australia saw a decline in cybercrimes in 2025, with fewer financial losses and online abuse, but small businesses experienced severe consequences, including legal and staffing issues. The Australian Institute of Criminology attributed this trend to protection shifting towards technology platforms, telcos, and devices, with individual precautions like antivirus software losing importance.

Student Loan Breach Exposes 2.5M Records

Severity: 8/10

A data breach by Nelnet Servicing exposed the personal data of 2.5 million student loan account holders, including names, home addresses, social security numbers, and contact information. The breach occurred between June 1 and July 22, 2022, and was discovered on July 21, with further information accessed by an unauthorized party.

Cybercriminals Are Selling Access to Chinese Surveillance Cameras

Severity: 8/10

Over 80,000 Hikvision surveillance cameras worldwide remain unpatched, exposing thousands of organizations to a 11-month-old critical command injection flaw. Despite its severe vulnerability rating of 9.8, the cameras' owners have not updated the software, leaving them vulnerable to potential hackers and threats from groups like Chinese and Russian threat actors.

Papa Johns Surveillance-Based Advertising

Severity: 6/10

Papa Johns is using data from Instacart and NBCUniversal to target consumers who might be low on groceries. They create a custom audience based on grocery purchases, then serve ads on streaming content with phrases like "Light on groceries?" or "Empty fridge?" to appear non-creepy, but some see this as an invasion of privacy.
