Cybersecurity Intelligence Brief

1. First VPN Dismantled in Global Takedown Over Use by 25 Ransomware Groups
Threat: Data Breach | Severity: 7/10
Authorities in Europe and North America have dismantled a VPN service, First VPN, used by criminals for anonymous ransomware attacks, data theft, and other illicit activities. Led by France and the Netherlands, the international operation involved multiple countries and seized 33 servers, infrastructure, and domains. The service was promoted on cybercrime forums and had connections in 27 countries, with at least 25 ransomware groups using it.

2. Ghostwriter Targets Ukraine Government Entities with Prometheus Phishing Malware
Threat: Ransomware | Severity: 8/10
Belarus-aligned threat actor Ghostwriter targets Ukrainian government organizations with phishing emails containing a PDF attachment linked to a ZIP archive. The payload, dubbed OYSTERFRESH, collects system information and is designed to lead to a final payload of Cobalt Strike, a widely abused post-exploitation framework. The attack aims to reduce the attack surface by restricting wscript.exe execution for standard user accounts.

3. Megalodon GitHub Attack Targets 5,561 Repos with Malicious CI/CD Workflows
Threat: Malware | Severity: 9

Explanation: This news refers to a targeted attack on GitHub repositories, which may indicate sophisticated and organized malicious activity. A large number (5,561 repos) is also affected, which could lead to potential widespread damage. However, the severity rating can be subject to interpretation based on available information and the specifics of the attack./10
Cybersecurity researchers uncovered a massive automated campaign named Megalodon that pushed 5,718 malicious commits to 5,561 GitHub repositories in under 6 hours, exfiltrating sensitive data and CI secrets. The attack mimicked routine CI maintenance and used throwaway accounts and forged identities, leaving a trail of compromised repositories and a warning that we have entered a new era of supply chain attacks.

4. Making Vulnerable Drivers Exploitable Without Hardware - The BYOVD Perspective
Threat: Vulnerability | Severity: 8/10
Researchers have developed a method to determine if Windows kernel mode drivers are exploitable even without the original hardware they were designed for. This could lead to Local Privilege Escalation or BYOVD (Bring Your Own Vulnerable Driver) attacks, disrupting system defenses like EDR components, which can grant attackers arbitrary access or resource abuse. The analysis focuses on device objects and how they can be targeted from user mode.

5. Kimwolf DDoS Botnet Operator Arrested in Canada Over DDoS-for-Hire Attacks
Threat: Data Breach | Severity: 6

(This rating is based on the fact that a botnet operator being arrested may decrease the immediate threat level, but the underlying issue of the DDoS-for-hire attacks remains a concern)/10
The US Department of Justice arrested a 23-year-old Canadian man, Jacob Butler, for allegedly operating the DDoS botnet "Kimwolf." Butler is accused of enslaving devices, selling access to other cybercriminals, and forcing them to participate in massive DDoS attacks worldwide. If convicted, he faces up to 10 years in prison for aiding and abetting computer intrusion.
