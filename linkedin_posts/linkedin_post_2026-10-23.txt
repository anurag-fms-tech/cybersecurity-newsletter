Cybersecurity Intelligence Brief

1. The AI Agent Nobody Owns: Watch Expert Webinar On Your Newest Insider Threat
Threat: Malware | Severity: 2/10
An unnoticed AI agent on a corporate laptop can store API keys, passwords and access internal systems without oversight—effectively an insider threat. While most AI‑security discussions focus on external attackers, the real risk often lies in “shadow AI” already inside the network that security tools can’t detect. A Bitdefender webinar will present a playbook for governing and controlling these hidden AI agents.

2. Arista Warns of Critical VeloCloud SQL Injection Zero-Day Exploited in Attacks
Threat: Zero-Day | Severity: 10/10
Arista warns that a zero‑day SQL‑injection (CVE‑2026‑114625) in its VeloCloud Orchestrator lets anyone with a logged‑in, even read‑only, account seize operator privileges and full control of SD‑WAN management. The bug, rated 9.9 CVSS, requires web‑interface access and a valid login. Patches are in VCO 5.2.3.18, 6.1.3.10 and 6.4.2.12; Hosted/Dedicated instances are already fixed. This is the third VCO exploit reported by Arista, the first requiring authentication.

3. eMudhra to Exit Global SSL/TLS Business After 8 Intermediate CA Keys Found Easily Guessable
Threat: Vulnerability | Severity: 9/10
Indian CA eMudhra will exit its global SSL/TLS business after a researcher recovered private keys for eight intermediate certificates, six of which protected websites. Chrome will drop trust for its ECC root on Oct. 27, and Firefox already did on Oct. 20, affecting ~30,800 certificates—mostly issued via InCommon for higher‑education sites. eMudhra will replace affected certs with free partner‑issued ones, keep revocation services running until all are expired or replaced, and retain other services. The SSL/TLS unit contributed roughly 1% of revenue.

4. ThreatsDay: AI Secrets Leak, PingCAP Source Theft, Signal Hang-Up Flaw, Laravel Auth Bypass + 21 More
Threat: Vulnerability | Severity: 8/10
This week saw a cascade of cyber threats: critical PHP, GitLab, Linux, Node.js and LiteLLM RCE bugs, compromised eMudhra certificates, and hundreds of hijacked Tor relays, plus stolen passwords and exposed databases. Bluesky flagged a phishing scam hitting 4,500+ users; Singapore police warned of a senior‑targeted APK scam costing S$989k. Andromeda, Roundcube and Genesys reported credential leaks and patches, though services remained intact.

5. Two CVSS 9.0 Linux KSMBD RCE Flaws Disclosed on Public Mailing Lists Before Kernel Fixes
Threat: Vulnerability | Severity: 10/10
Linux’s built‑in SMB server (KSMBD) has two publicly disclosed RCE bugs, each rated 9.0 by Trend Micro’s ZDI. ZDI‑CAN‑33774 is an unauthenticated race that lets attackers craft SMB1/SMB2 packet mixes to overwrite memory. ZDI‑CAN‑33549 is an integer underflow in the SMB Direct (RDMA) path that can overrun buffers if the server offers a writable share and uses RDMA hardware or the rxe driver. No patches or CVEs exist yet; a fix for the RDMA issue is pending in the next kernel cycle, while mainline remediation may take weeks. Red Hat Enterprise Linux does not ship KSMBD by default.
