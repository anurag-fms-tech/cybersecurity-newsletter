Cybersecurity Intelligence Brief

1. China-Linked JDY Botnet Expands to 1,500+ Devices for Cyber Reconnaissance
Threat: Nation State | Severity: 8/10
A covert network called JDY, associated with China-nexus state-sponsored threat actors, has resurfaced with over 1,500 compromised devices, including SOHO and IoT devices. The network operates as a high-performance scanner for broad reconnaissance, primarily targeting internet targets, and has expanded to evade traditional IP-based defenses. Its size has grown from 650 bots to over 1,500, with most compromised devices located in the U.S., Brazil, Europe, and Asia.

2. Ivanti, Fortinet, and SAP Release Patches for Multiple Critical Vulnerabilities
Threat: Vulnerability | Severity: 8/10
Fortinet, Ivanti, and SAP released security updates to address critical vulnerabilities. Fortinet patched a CVE-2026-25089 command injection flaw in FortiSandbox, Ivanti fixed two flaws in Ivanti Sentry, and SAP addressed four vulnerabilities in NetWeaver AS ABAP, ABAP Platform, SAP Commerce Cloud, and Data Hub, impacting potential arbitrary code execution and information disclosure.

3. Unpatched Langflow Flaw CVE-2026-5027 Exploited for Unauthenticated RCE
Threat: Vulnerability | Severity: 10/10
A high-severity unpatched security flaw (CVE-2026-5027) in Langflow, an AI application platform, has been actively exploited. The vulnerability allows attackers to write files to arbitrary locations, potentially enabling remote code execution, due to unsanitized input and default unauthenticated auto-login. Approximately 7,000 Langflow instances are publicly exposed, with most located in North America, and have been targeted in exploitation efforts.

4. CISA Adds Cisco, Chrome, and Arista Flaws to KEV Catalog Amid Active Exploitation
Threat: Vulnerability | Severity: 8/10
The US CISA added three vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list, including CVE-2026-7473 in Arista's EOS, which can be exploited to forward unexpected tunneled packets. Arista acknowledges exploitation and offers mitigation steps, but cites risks with issuing patches that could break existing configurations. FCEB agencies are ordered to apply fixes or mitigations by June 23, 2026.

5. Your Automated Pentest Looks Clean. See What It Missed in This Expert Webinar
Threat: Vulnerability | Severity: 6/10
Automated pentesting may give a "stable" report, but security risks persist. The tool validates attack paths, but not other aspects of security, such as detection rules, cloud configurations, and SIEM/EDR efficacy. This gap can be closed with breach and attack simulation, which assesses control reactions to known behaviors, rather than focusing solely on exploitable paths.
