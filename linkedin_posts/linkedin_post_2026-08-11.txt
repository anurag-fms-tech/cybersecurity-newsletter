Threat: Ransomware | Severity: 8

Explanation:
This news involves a new piece of ransomware ("StormEncryptor") attributed to China-linked hackers, as well as a potential vulnerability in N-central (implying a compromised system or service). It suggests a targeted attack that could potentially result in data breaches and monetary losses, thereby warranting a high severity rating./10
Microsoft has disclosed that China-based threat actor Storm-1175 has deployed a new ransomware strain called StormEncryptor, marking a shift from previous use of Medusa ransomware. StormEncryptor exploits a vulnerability in N-able N-central, allowing the adversary to obtain initial access. Affected users are advised to apply patches as soon as possible to avoid high-velocity attacks by the financially motivated threat actor.

4. Kimsuky Builds Offline AI Stack to Boost Phishing and Automate Malware Development
Threat: Vulnerability | Severity: 8/10
North Korea's Kimsuky hacking unit has begun to integrate artificial intelligence into its malware by assembling and testing existing tools, allowing for quicker and harder-to-spot attacks. South Korean security firm Genians discovered the setup after tracking infrastructure tied to the group, and recommends defenders to focus on correlating suspicious activity rather than judging lures by their appearance.

5. New Passkey Attacks Can Recover Synced Private Keys or Bypass Phishing-Resistant MFA
Threat: Vulnerability | Severity: 8/10
Three separate research groups found vulnerabilities in passkey protections, not the cryptography they rely on. The attacks exploited exposed signed authentication material on Windows, reused passkey material from compromised systems, or used compromised user sessions for Windows Hello. Microsoft has addressed the logging vulnerability CVE-2026-34348 and applied mitigations for passkey relay assertions, recommending a least-privilege access approach and Zero Trust model to protect against these attacks.
