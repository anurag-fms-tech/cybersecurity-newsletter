Cybersecurity Intelligence Brief

1. TeamPCP Compromises Checkmarx Jenkins AST Plugin Weeks After KICS Supply Chain Attack
Threat: Vulnerability | Severity: 8/10
Checkmarx confirms a modified Jenkins AST plugin was published to the Jenkins Marketplace, exploiting a vulnerability targeting developer secrets. Attackers, linked to TeamPCP, gained unauthorized access to the plugin's GitHub repository, renaming it and defacing its description. Checkmarx has released a patched version (2.0.13-848), and users must ensure they are using the correct version (2.0.13-829 or later) or update to 2.0.13-848.

2. cPanel CVE-2026-41940 Under Active Exploitation to Deploy Filemanager Backdoor
Threat: Vulnerability | Severity: 9

(Note: The severity of this CVE is difficult to assess without knowing the impact, complexity, or ease of exploitation. However, based on the information provided (CVE being actively exploited), we can infer significant potential for security breaches, resulting in a high severity rating.)/10
Cybersecurity researchers attribute the exploitation of a critical cPanel flaw to a threat actor named Mr_Rot13, deploying a backdoor called Filemanager on compromised environments. The vulnerability, CVE-2026-41940, allows remote attackers to gain control, resulting in malicious activities like cryptocurrency mining, ransomware, and backdoor implantation. Over 2,000 attacker IPs worldwide are involved in automated attacks targeting this vulnerability.

3. Hackers Used AI to Develop First Known Zero-Day 2FA Bypass for Mass Exploitation
Threat: Zero-Day | Severity: 10/10
Google identified a threat actor using a zero-day exploit likely developed with an AI system, marking its first known malicious use. The tech giant described it as a "mass vulnerability exploitation operation." Although it's unclear if Google's Gemini AI tool was used, the exploit featured characteristics of large language model-generated code, highlighting AI's acceleration of vulnerability discovery and faster exploitation timelines.

5. Your Purple Team Isn't Purple — It's Just Red and Blue in the Same Room
Threat: Vulnerability | Severity: 1

This news appears to be more about team dynamics and naming conventions rather than a serious cybersecurity threat./10
Cybersecurity defenses are falling behind attackers. The gap between a security vulnerability being published and a working exploit shrinking from 56 days to just 10 hours. Purple teaming, a practice that brings red (attackers) and blue (defenders) teams together to continuously tighten security posture, is a good solution but rarely executed effectively due to human communication and meeting issues.
